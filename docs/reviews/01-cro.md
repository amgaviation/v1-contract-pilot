- Issue: `README.md` states plainly: "signup returns `Error sending confirmation email`. SMTP is configured against Resend, credentials are accepted, and the send is rejected with `550 — the sending domain is not verified`... no new pilot can complete signup" until `amgaviationgroup.com` is verified at Resend, or the sender is temporarily pointed at `onboarding@resend.dev`. Every CTA reviewed in this report — hero, close band, all three pricing cards, the pricing closing band — routes to `/signup`, which cannot presently hand a real visitor a session. No copy change on any page in scope changes this: the entire funnel this review evaluates is currently a dead end for a real prospect.
- Fix: Not a marketing-copy fix — this is a Resend domain-verification/DNS action (DKIM + SPF at resend.com/domains for `amgaviationgroup.com`), or a temporary sender swap to `onboarding@resend.dev`, outside this review's file scope. Flagged here because it overrides the priority of every other finding below until resolved: no hero, CTA, or objection-handling fix can convert a visitor while this holds.

### [HIGH] The landing page blocks its own first paint behind a session round-trip, against the strategy's own "ten seconds on FBO wifi" bar
- Where: `app/(marketing)/page.tsx:321-324`; `app/(marketing)/loading.tsx:1-23`; `lib/supabase/account.ts:115-120`
- Issue: `LandingPage` is an async Server Component that does `const ctx = await getSessionContext();` before returning any JSX (`page.tsx:322`). `getSessionContext()` calls `supabase.auth.getUser()` (`lib/supabase/account.ts:119`), a network round trip to the Supabase Auth server, for every visitor, before the hero H1, subhead, or CTA can render. `loading.tsx`'s own header comment confirms this is exactly what it exists to cover: "Only app/(marketing)/page.tsx actually awaits anything (the signed-in redirect check)." The redirect only ever fires for a visitor who is already signed in — the overwhelming majority of first-touch marketing traffic has no session at all and gains nothing from the check, yet pays its latency on every load, and on genuinely bad FBO wifi that round trip is exactly the kind of delay the strategy's own budget (`docs/MARKETING.md` §6, "ten seconds on FBO wifi") is written against. `/pricing`, `/terms`, `/privacy` do not do this (they are static) — this is specific to the page carrying the primary hero and CTA.
- Fix: Skip the round trip for the common case: check for the presence of the Supabase auth cookie (a synchronous read, no network call) before calling `getSessionContext()`, and render the marketing page directly when it is absent — reserve the `getUser()`/redirect check for the visitor who actually has one.

### [HIGH] The CTA promises "Try," the next screens deliver an identity form, a mandatory email round-trip, then a plan/price screen
- Where: `app/(marketing)/page.tsx:358-363` ("Try V1 — $5 first month" → `/signup`); `app/(auth)/signup/actions.ts:158-160`; `app/(auth)/welcome/page.tsx:84-95`
- Issue: The hero and close-band CTAs read "Try V1 — $5 first month," which reads as a fast, one-step start on an offer whose price is already stated. What actually follows: a 5-field identity form (name, base, account type, email, password) on `/signup`, then — per `signup/actions.ts:158-160`, "When email confirmation is enabled on the Supabase project, signUp returns a user with no session... the pilot must click the link before they can do anything" — a forced context switch out of the browser into an email client, and only after clicking that link does the visitor reach `/welcome`, where plan selection and the Stripe card entry actually happen (`welcome/page.tsx:86-89`, "Pick your plan... Your account starts the moment checkout completes"). The gap between "Try" and a four-hop, mailbox-interrupting path to an actual plan/price screen is a classic drop-off point, independent of the copy quality on any single screen along the way.
- Fix: Nothing in scope can remove the email-confirmation requirement (that is a Supabase Auth project setting, not marketing copy), but the CTA's own promise can be brought closer to what happens next — e.g. swap "Try V1" for language that sets up an account-creation step rather than an immediate trial ("Create your account" / "Set up your books"), reserving "Try" framing for the point where a plan and price are actually chosen.

### [HIGH] The homepage has no page-specific title; it inherits the bare word "V1"
- Where: `app/layout.tsx:9-12` (`title: { default: BRAND.name, template: `%s | ${BRAND.name}` }`, i.e. `default: "V1"`); absence confirmed — no `export const metadata` anywhere in `app/(marketing)/page.tsx` (grepped; `terms/page.tsx:13`, `privacy/page.tsx:11`, and `pricing/page.tsx:47` all declare their own, the landing page alone does not)
- Issue: Per the resolution mechanism `app/(marketing)/layout.tsx:41-46` itself documents ("Next fills both from each page's RESOLVED metadata when the blocks omit them"), a segment that declares no `title` inherits the root's bare `default` string, not a templated one — so the homepage's browser-tab title and SERP title are the single word "V1," with no descriptor at all. "V1" is one of the most overloaded terms in this exact audience's own vocabulary — it is the takeoff decision speed every pilot searches, drills, and talks about constantly — so a bare "V1" result competes for attention against a flood of unrelated aviation-training content in the one channel (organic search, by pilots, about flying) this product should have a natural edge in.
- Fix: Give the landing page its own `metadata.title`, sourced from `lib/brand.ts` (e.g. built from `BRAND.tagline`, "The books for your flying business."), so the rendered title differentiates the result instead of relying on the bare brand default.

### [HIGH] The trust cost of `/terms` and `/privacy`: a card-required product's own legal pages say nothing on them is binding
- Where: `app/(marketing)/terms/page.tsx:63-74`; `app/(marketing)/privacy/page.tsx:59-69`; linked on every marketing page via `app/(marketing)/site-footer.tsx:41-46` (Legal column) and again on the signup form's submit path per `app/(auth)/signup/signup-form.tsx:239-249` ("See our Terms and Privacy Policy")

This is a launch-readiness gap, not a copy defect: the real fix is completing the counsel review before driving paid acquisition at volume. Surfacing this plainly rather than proposing a copy workaround, per the rule that a fix violating the claim rules is invalid.
