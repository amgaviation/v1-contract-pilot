# What V1 takes from FlightDeptPro — functionality only

Companion to [`FLIGHTDEPTPRO-AUDIT.md`](./FLIGHTDEPTPRO-AUDIT.md). This document contains
**zero UI, layout, or brand observations** — by instruction. Everything here is a mechanism,
a data-model idea, or a workflow, filtered through three rules:

1. **It must serve one independent contract pilot**, not a flight department. Anything
   department-scale is listed in §D as explicitly rejected, so it doesn't creep back in.
2. **It must strengthen the V1 thesis** — *log the trip once → logbook entry, invoice line,
   expense file all post from it* (`docs/PLAN.md`). FlightDeptPro is the strongest validation
   of that thesis we've seen: it runs the same log-once loop from the opposite side of the
   table (they *receive* the contract pilot's invoice; our user *sends* it), and its single
   worst structural failure is the one place it broke the loop (pilot hours never flow — their
   crew-hours report reads 0.0 forever).
3. **It must not violate a locked decision** (draft-confirm logbook, currency behind a flag,
   no marketplace, no AMG visibility, Stripe Connect Standard).

Sections: A — adopt as designed. B — adapt with changes. C — their failures converted into V1
requirements. D — explicitly not taking.

---

## A. Adopt (mapped to the V1 plan)

### A1. One "expiring thing" engine with an escalation ladder → Documents + Currency board (Phases 7–8)
Their best piece of infrastructure: every date-bearing item (medical, 61.58, recurrent
training, registration, passenger passports, generic documents) reduces to
*item + expiry + owning entity*, and one engine fires reminders at **T-30 / T-14 / T-7 / T-1 /
OVERDUE**, each deep-linked to the owning record, surfaced in a bell feed, a notifications
page, and the dashboard queue, with red/expired vs amber/within-30-days states.
**V1:** implement `expirations` as one engine over `documents` (medical, flight review,
passport, certificates, W-9) and currency snapshot outputs. Same ladder, every alert
deep-links, everything also lands in the Overview "Needs Attention" queue. One engine, all
modules — see C4 for the wiring rule they broke.

### A2. Trip as the financial atom with a margin rollup → Trips (Phase 3, 5)
Every trip answers "did this job make money" on the trip page itself: money in − money out =
margin, with each figure hyperlinked to its artifact (invoice, expense lines, reimbursables),
and pass-through costs explicitly excluded ("not in your margin").
**V1:** the trip page shows `billable (days × rate + rebilled expenses) − deducted expenses =
trip net`, linking the invoice and each expense. The pilot's version of "margin" is *earned vs
out-of-pocket*, and rebill-vs-deduct already models their pass-through distinction. This is
V1's data model made visible; adopt the rollup-with-linked-artifacts mechanic exactly.

### A3. One expense classification driving everything downstream → Expenses (Phase 4)
Their single cleanest idea: a payer/treatment tag set at capture (company card / client's card /
personal card + re-bill / reimbursable) drives margin inclusion, which report variant a line
appears on, the reimbursement queue, the client statement, and P&L separation — one tag, five+
consequences, never re-asked.
**V1 already locked `treatment ('rebill'|'deduct'|'unassigned')`** — this confirms the design.
Adopt the discipline: treatment is asked **once at capture**, and every downstream surface
(invoice draft, deduction file, year-end packet, client-facing expense report) derives from it.
No surface ever re-asks.

### A4. Config-once rates on the client record → Clients (Phase 3)
Day rates and per-diem spreads live on the operator (client) record and multiply into trips,
statements, and payables automatically — never re-entered per trip.
**V1:** `clients.default_day_rate` and payment terms are already planned; add optional
`default_per_diem` and default expense treatment per client. Trip creation pre-fills from the
client; the pilot overrides per trip only when the deal differs.

### A5. Auto-drafted counterparty paperwork from trip facts → Invoices (Phase 5)
Crew assignment materializes the pilot's bill with zero data entry: `CP-{trip}-{seq}`,
auto-description ("2 contract pilot days for D-0003"), day rate, net-15 due date, overdue
badges, state tabs. That document *is our user's invoice*, generated by the counterparty's
system.
**V1:** invoice drafting from a trip must produce at least this quality automatically — line
per day-block with a human description naming the trip and dates, plus one line per rebilled
expense, client terms applied, sequential numbering (already planned via a sequence table).
The pilot reviews and sends; nothing is sent silently (mirrors the logbook draft-confirm rule).
Overdue state with day counts feeds the Needs Attention queue.

### A6. Client-facing expense report variants → Expenses/Invoices (Phases 4–5)
Per-trip expense reports render in distinct audiences from the same rows: internal copy vs
client copy, with reimbursable vs charged sections and per-category subtotals, as print and
PDF.
**V1:** generate a **client copy** expense report per trip (rebilled lines + receipts) as an
invoice attachment, and an **internal copy** (everything, including deducted lines) for the
pilot's own records and the accountant packet. Same rows, treatment tag decides placement.

### A7. Days-remaining currency presentation + typed action queue → Overview (Phase 7)
Currency items carry a computed **days-remaining countdown** per item, and the dashboard
merges everything into one triaged queue ("4 urgent, 6 soon") with typed tags and deep links.
**V1:** the Currency & Expirations board shows days-remaining per row alongside the locked
status vocabulary (`estimated_current / estimated_not_current / insufficient_data`), and the
Needs Attention queue gets urgency buckets (urgent / soon) and type tags (currency, past-due
invoice, unassigned receipt, W-9 outstanding) with the everything-deep-links rule. All of this
stays behind the currency flag where it derives from currency; the disclaimer travels with the
data (locked).

### A8. Completeness loops that chase the user → Logbook (Phase 6)
"9 flown legs not logged yet — open the trip's Flight log to record them," each linking the
exact entry form. The product, not discipline, closes the bookkeeping gap. Same pattern in
reverse for money: unbilled work surfaced until invoiced.
**V1:** two standing queues — completed trips with unconfirmed logbook drafts, and completed
trips not yet fully invoiced — both in Needs Attention, both deep-linking the confirm/draft
screens. (The mockup's "Ready to Invoice" list is exactly this; extend the same loop to the
logbook side.)

### A9. Running-total context at entry time → Logbook (Phase 6)
Their leg-log form shows carry-forward math live ("Airframe 4864.0 → 4866.5") and a
before/+this/after table against a settable baseline.
**V1:** logbook entry (manual or trip-derived draft confirmation) shows the affected running
totals (total time, PIC, night, instrument, landings) before/after the entry, computed from a
baseline the pilot sets at import/onboarding. Cheap to build once totals exist, and it catches
fat-finger hours at the moment of entry.

### A10. Airport-code-derived timezones → Trips (Phase 3)
"No time-zone setting is needed" — every leg time displays in that airport's local zone,
derived from the code, with Zulu alongside.
**V1:** trip legs already carry from/to ICAO and out/in times; store UTC, display
airport-local + Zulu derived from the ICAO. No tz picker anywhere in the product.

### A11. CSV conventions → Logbook import (Phase 6) and every list
Every list has a matching CSV export; imports ship a commented template where example rows are
ignored on import and phone numbers are normalized.
**V1:** the ForeFlight/LogTen/generic import already planned adopts the template conventions
(downloadable template, example rows ignored, normalization on ingest, rejected rows surfaced
for review — the error-review surface is already in the plan). Every V1 list view gets a CSV
export from day one; it's also the cheap half of the data-exit story (C6).

### A12. Type-DELETE confirmation → everywhere destructive
Destructive deletes require typing DELETE, button disabled until it matches. Adopt verbatim
for deleting clients, trips, invoices, logbook entries, and import batches.

### A13. Read-only seeded demo as go-to-market → launch scope
A fully seeded, read-only demo reachable without signup is the whole top of their funnel and
it works — it's how this audit existed at all. **V1:** ship a `/demo` with a synthetic pilot
(seed script, no live data — standing gate respected) once Phases 3–5 are demonstrable. With
one hard rule from their failure: the demo must let an evaluator watch the **complete
log-once loop** (trip → invoice draft → expense file → logbook draft) — see C5.

## B. Adapt with changes

### B1. Their owner statement → the V1 **client statement** (Phase 8)
Aircraft × month → one artifact: activity summary, per-trip totals, money due, coming-due
items, notes — print/PDF/CSV/email, recipients managed on the client record. That is the
single most client-retentive artifact in their product.
**V1 adaptation:** *client* × period → statement for the pilot's client: trips flown with day
counts, invoiced vs paid vs outstanding, rebilled expense summary. And the same engine pointed
inward becomes the **accountant packet** (Phase 8): year × pilot → income by client, deductible
expenses by category, logbook totals. Skip: budget-vs-actual columns and management fees
(department constructs).
**Change:** exclude unflown/future trips from client-facing statements — theirs leaks a
CONFIRMED future trip at $0.00 onto the owner's bill.

### B2. Their pay-run instructions → V1 collections nudges (Phase 5)
Their reimbursements page resolves owed amounts into literal instructions ("Pay Danny Ortiz
$340.00") per period. The shape — *output formatted as the action the user takes next* — is
the part to keep.
**V1 adaptation:** the pilot's receivables view resolves into per-client actions: "Send
reminder to XYZ Corp — INV-0007, 12 days overdue, $4,800" with the reminder email one click
away (platform-send path already locked). Same shape, opposite direction of money.

### B3. Their readiness gates → V1 billing/logbook completeness gates (Phases 5–6)
A 13-point per-trip readiness audit with countable state ("3 of 13 ready") works because gates
are explicit and enumerable. V1 must not absorb operational go/no-go gates (that's the
operator's world and a liability surface we deliberately avoid), but the *mechanic* maps to
paperwork: a trip's completeness state — legs entered, expenses assigned, invoice drafted/sent/
paid, logbook confirmed, W-9 on file — as an enumerable checklist driving the queues in A8.
"Paperwork complete 4/6" per trip, nothing about airworthiness, ever.

### B4. Their email-in inbox with parsed fields → V1 receipt intake, later and narrower (post-Phase 4)
One forwarding address ingests receipts and parses vendor/amount/date/category into a review
queue. For a solo pilot the *photo-at-capture* moment matters more than email forwarding, so:
Phase 4 ships receipt file upload into the **unassigned queue** (already first-class in the
plan); a parse-assist step (photo/email → suggested vendor/amount/date/category, pilot
confirms) is a fast-follow. Never auto-post a parsed receipt — same draft-confirm principle as
the logbook. Their inbox's Needs Review → Processed lifecycle is the right state model for it.

### B5. Their two-tier AI posture → a V1 posture note only
What's right: grounded Q&A separated from a role-gated agent whose writes are propose-confirm
("asks before it acts"), and provenance labels on suggestions ("verify independently").
**V1:** no AI features in v1 scope, and their grounding failure (§C2) is the reason to keep it
that way. If an assistant ever ships, it inherits the propose-confirm rule that already
governs trip→logbook, and it is grounded in *every* module it will answer about before it
answers about any — a launch gate, not an aspiration.

### B6. People records decoupled from logins → V1 business accounts (Phase 1)
Their crew list holds people who are "records here only to be scheduled," not app users.
V1's business-account kind has the same need: a two-pilot LLC where one seat does the books —
crew names can appear on trips/logbook shares without being members. `account_members` stays
the auth model (locked identity model); trip participants are data, not identities.

### B7. Their honest-provenance habit → V1 copy discipline everywhere
"est" markers with the fallback rule stated, "informational, not a record of compliance,"
basis footnotes on money reports, and a liability line on the login page ("organizes and
flags… decisions remain with the operator, PIC, and mechanic"). V1's currency disclaimer is
already locked and counsel-gated; extend the *pattern*: logbook totals label their source mix
(`trip` / `import` / `manual` — the `source` column already planned), currency shows its
computed-from basis, money reports state their basis (issue-date vs paid-date — pick one,
state it, keep it consistent, which is also lesson C3).

## C. Their failures, converted into V1 requirements

These are the audit's "wrong / missing / business-killer" findings restated as things V1 must
do. Each is cheap now and expensive later.

1. **No silent write failures, ever.** Their most labor-intensive form returns HTTP 200,
   persists nothing, and shows nothing. V1 rule: every mutation ends in a visible success or a
   visible, specific failure; a blocked write says *why*. Belongs in the house verify scripts
   (`trip:verify` asserting error surfacing, not just happy paths).
2. **Cross-module invariants get verify scripts.** Their P&L shows −$50k while their own
   payments ledger shows ~$580k collected, their fleet card says READY while the alert engine
   says overdue, their AI denies what the dashboard flags. V1 already has the verify-script
   convention — add invariant checks across module boundaries: invoice totals = trip billables,
   logbook totals = confirmed entries, currency board = documents + snapshots, queue counts =
   queryable rows. Two sources for one number is a defect.
3. **One money basis, stated.** Their revenue basis (invoice issue-date) silently diverges
   from their cash records. V1 reports pick a basis per report, print it on the report, and
   reconcile: Awaiting Payment (invoice basis) + Paid This Year (cash basis) must be derivable
   from the same rows.
4. **The expiry engine covers *everything* with a date — enforced.** Their ladder fires for
   crew docs but never for the two expired compliance programs (different module, never
   wired). V1: any table carrying an expiry participates in the A1 engine by construction —
   one code path, and a verify script asserting no date-bearing row type is outside it.
5. **The demo (and trial) must demonstrate the paid core.** Their demo feature-flags off
   invoicing — the thing the dashboard advertises — and walls off every paid differentiator.
   V1's trial is full-featured on synthetic-friendly rails (Stripe test-mode Connect in the
   demo account; card-required trial already locked for real accounts). Nobody should have to
   *imagine* the invoice PDF.
6. **Data exit is a feature.** No API, partial exports, no import for most entities — in a
   records product. V1 ships full-fidelity export (logbook CSV round-trips through our own
   generic importer, invoices as PDF + CSV, expenses with receipt files) from the phase each
   record type ships in. "Your records leave with you" is a trust feature a *powered-by-AMG*
   product especially needs, and the accountant packet already implies most of it.
7. **Don't sell what isn't built.** Their pricing page lists e-signature, SMS reporting, and
   tail-scoped roles that don't observably exist, next to draft Terms on broken pages. V1's
   marketing lists shipped features only; Terms/Privacy are counsel-reviewed before revenue
   (already a standing gate). In a word-of-mouth pilot market this is existential.
8. **Price the collaboration axis correctly.** Their Basic tier gives a *team* product one
   shared login; teams pay 50% more per aircraft to log in separately. V1's locked model —
   solo flat rate, business per-seat — is already the right shape. Hold the line: never gate
   basic collaboration (a bookkeeper seat) behind a tier jump designed for something else.
9. **Working offline-adjacent beats claiming PWA.** Their manifest-only PWA has no service
   worker and zero offline capability for users on ramps with bad LTE. V1 v1 doesn't promise
   offline; but the trip log/receipt capture forms — the two things a pilot does *at the
   airplane* — should degrade gracefully (draft state survives a dropped connection) before
   anything is called an app.
10. **Tax is not optional on invoices.** They have a Part 135 billing mode with no FET and pay
    contractors with no 1099 concept. V1's user has the mirror needs: invoice lines must
    support tax where a pilot's state/situation requires it, the W-9 workflow (already in the
    plan) exists precisely because clients will 1099 the pilot, and the accountant packet is
    the year-end answer. Counsel reviews the tax copy (existing gate); the schema supports it
    from Phase 5, not retrofitted.

## D. Explicitly not taking (scope guard)

Department-scale features that would dilute the product and violate the plan's boundaries —
listed so they don't creep in via "FlightDeptPro has it":

- Maintenance tracking, squawks/MEL, inspections, aircraft times-and-cycles as a maintenance
  record. V1 tracks the pilot's flights, not airworthiness.
- Trip readiness / release / FRAT / W&B / fit-duty gates — operational control surfaces.
  V1's gates are paperwork gates only (B3). The liability posture is locked: planning aid,
  never a go/no-go determination.
- Crew scheduling, duty/rest advisory, multi-pilot rosters, qualified-crew matrices.
- Owner/aircraft-management constructs: management fees, owner statements *for aircraft
  owners*, trust accounting, budget-vs-actual, dispatch-reliability analytics.
- Passenger manifests, eAPIS, catering preferences, GSE, contract-fuel programs, vendor
  directories, department checklists, SMS/compliance registers.
- An AI assistant at launch (B5).
- Anything marketplace-shaped. Their "Operators" directory is *their* client book; V1's
  clients are the pilot's — and no path surfaces them to AMG (locked, tested by
  `tenancy:verify`).

---

*One-line summary: FlightDeptPro proves the log-once thesis from the buyer's side of the
table, hands V1 a working blueprint for the expiry engine, the trip-margin rollup, the
treatment-tag pipeline, auto-drafted invoices, client statements, and completeness queues —
and its failures write V1's quality bar: no silent writes, one source per number, full data
exit, sell only what ships.*
